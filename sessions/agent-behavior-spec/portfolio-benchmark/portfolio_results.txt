1. PRO-CHANGE / "the proposed change wins" BIAS. The sim predicted TREATMENT on 7 of 8
   cases (every case except duolingo). It got all 5 control-won counter-intuitive cases
   WRONG (predicted the change) and scored only on the 2 treatment-won cases where the
   change genuinely won. The one case it called "control" (duolingo) was actually
   treatment-won -> wrong too. So the sim is directionally near-degenerate toward "the
   change wins", which is WORSE than the naive "always-treatment" baseline (which would
   score 3/8 = 0.375 on this set; the sim scored 2/8). This is priors-echoing, the exact
   failure mode the balanced counter-intuitive portfolio was designed to expose.
   - MIRROR-IMAGE of the Hillstrom ad-aversion bias: in marketing-NUDGE framing the sim
     predicts the treatment HARMS (anti-change); in product-CHANGE framing it predicts the
     treatment WINS (pro-change). Both are echoing the prior the framing evokes, not
     reasoning about the deliberative trade-off. The common root: introspective elicitation
     on a blinded single-state view echoes the salient prior of the framing.

   WRONG here (generic "conversion" elicitation) but RIGHT in the dedicated retention-
   specific pilot (by-arm retention_7 dir_acc 1.0, see BENCHMARK_REPORT.md sec 6). The
   single generic-conversion elicitation under-fits a case whose real mechanism is a

   follow-up.
