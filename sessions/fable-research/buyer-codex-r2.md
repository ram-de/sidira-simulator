# Buyer Council Convergence Round 2

Inputs read: `sessions/fable-research/buyer-codex-r1.md` and `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md`.

This memo does not re-litigate the settled points: Amazon-class is a no; the wedge is a sealed winner's-curse audit of past exhaust, not prediction; the current irreproducible asset is not code or method; and the anti-pitch is mandatory.

## 1. Price Gap Ruling

Fable is closer for first-10-customer reality. My Round 1 price was too high for a pre-revenue two-founder company with zero case studies, an unproven demand trigger, and a first artifact that looks like a services-heavy audit rather than production software.

Ruling:

- Entry audit: **$25k-$50k fixed fee**, with permission to quote **$60k** only when the buyer has unusually rich exhaust, obvious executive sponsorship, and a concrete decision under review.
- Expansion: **$75k-$175k/year** for the first recurring watchdog/certificate subscription, not $250k-$750k/year.
- $200k+ ACV is not killed forever, but it requires at least one of: measured customer-side Arm-B coverage near/above the threshold, a renewal with a documented reversed/skipped decision worth more than the fee, or a live tier-3 feature-store engagement.

Reasoning: the buyer is not yet buying a platform. They are buying a trust-building forensic artifact from founders with no references. Even if the theoretical value of one reversed bad launch is six or seven figures, procurement discounts unproven diagnostics hard. A $100k-$250k first audit asks the buyer to believe the category before Sidira has proved that buyers feel the pain. $25k-$50k is small enough to route through innovation, data, growth, or consulting budget, but still high enough to avoid unpaid research theater.

The right motion is not "cheap forever." It is **low-friction paid proof, then expansion only after the audit produces a buyer-recognized receipt**.

## 2. Demand Risk Ruling

Adopt Fable's red-team objection. This is the most important correction to Round 1.

Winner's curse often presents internally as success: the dashboard went green, the roadmap item launched, the champion got credit, and the organization moved on. An audit that says "your win was probably noise" can embarrass the exact person who would need to sponsor the purchase. That makes demand **fatal-unless-disproven**, not a secondary GTM caveat.

Amendment: the red-team repair is necessary but not sufficient. Dollar-denominated, forward-looking framing reduces the ego problem, but it still needs a buyer archetype with someone to blame other than the internal champion. The strongest first wedge is therefore:

- "This shipped win will miss the annualized plan by $X unless you run a cheap holdout."
- "Your agency/vendor/platform called this a win; we will independently certify whether it survives shrinkage and guardrails."
- "Before you roll this out to the rest of the portfolio, let's audit whether the evidence is real."

This changes the kill conditions. Add a hard demand kill:

1. **Pitch kill:** 3-5 on-profile paid-audit pitches using the repaired framing produce no paid conversion.
2. **Receipt kill:** an audit identifies a decision reversal or skipped test that the buyer agrees is economically material, but the buyer still will not renew, expand, refer, or approve a second audit.
3. **Champion kill:** the only excited users are DS critics, while budget owners consistently experience the report as blame rather than budget defense.

If these trigger, the product may still be analytically correct, but the wedge is commercially dead.

## 3. Third ICP Ruling

Pick **PE portfolio-operations teams** for year one over white-label platforms/consultancies.

Reasoning:

- PE port ops preserves the audit's independent-certifier posture. White-labeling through platforms or consultancies muddies the anti-pitch and risks turning Sidira into copy inside someone else's dashboard.
- PE creates one relationship with multiple company audits, which is exactly what Sidira needs for a sealed cross-customer corpus.
- The embarrassment vector is cleaner: the target is not "your PMs were dumb"; it is "before we scale this operating playbook across the portfolio, let's certify which wins are real."
- Platforms and consultancies have worse strategic leakage. Fable is right that platform absorption is already on an 18-24 month clock; embedding early with them accelerates feature-copy risk before Sidira owns enough certificates to matter.

White-label is not abandoned; it is a second-year distribution option after Sidira has its own proof library and can negotiate from strength.

## 4. Coverage Kill Threshold

Endorse Fable's **~40% customer-side Arm-B coverage** line, with a precise scope.

The 40% threshold applies to the **recurring decision-gate subscription**, not to the initial audit. The audit can sell below 40% if it produces high-value reversals in historical exhaust. But a recurring watchdog that can only call 10-20% of owned-pilot decisions will feel like a niche analyst report, not a renewal-worthy operating layer.

Kill or downgrade the subscription thesis if customer-owned per-arm exports on real backlog yield:

- **<30% coverage:** kill recurring gate positioning; sell only audit/triage unless value receipts are exceptional.
- **30-40% coverage:** probation zone; continue only with zero confident-wrong and strong economic receipts.
- **>=40% coverage:** subscription thesis is live if demand receipts also exist.

One confident-wrong sign in the wild is a separate severity event. It does not automatically kill all audit work, but it kills the "never confidently ship the wrong sign" sales claim until the admission rule is repaired and revalidated.

## 5. Hostile Pass On Fable Overclaims

- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:18` says "Weblab ships shrinkage natively." That may be directionally plausible, but the provided record does not prove the exact native Weblab feature state. Use "Amazon-class teams know and can implement shrinkage" unless externally verified.
- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:20` says "No vendor has ever cracked Weblab." This is rhetorically useful but too absolute without evidence. The decision does not need it; the build/security argument is enough.
- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:22` generalizes "any company with a central experimentation-science org is a non-buyer." Too broad. They are bad first-year targets, but a central org could buy after Sidira has sealed certificates, zero-egress deployment, and a niche they do not want to staff.
- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:26` calls the audit "day-one ROI." Overclaim. It is day-one *possibility* of ROI. The red-team demand objection exists precisely because the buyer may not recognize reversed past wins as value.
- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:42` says platforms "won't ship" refusal because of conflict of interest. We should weaken to "may under-ship or under-market." Platforms can absolutely ship shrinkage and abstention as trust features if customers demand them.
- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:48` says the revenge-purchase archetype "partially dissolves" the embarrassment problem. It helps, but it narrows the reachable market. It does not dissolve internal blame when the bad call was made by the buyer's own growth team.
- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:58` says the business is a race to accumulate sealed certificates before platforms ship the feature. Mostly right, but incomplete: the race is also to prove **demand**, not just accumulate evidence.
- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:70` says sell 3-5 paid audits "now." Correct if "paid" can include founder-led concierge delivery. Incorrect if it implies the product surface is already enough; the sales artifact must be a tight audit package, not the current simulator positioning.
- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:125` says the platform misalignment is "the actual moat." Too strong. It is temporary counter-positioning. The only plausible moat is the sealed certificate corpus plus repeatable zero-egress deployment, and both are prospective.
- `sessions/fable-research/buyer-council-fable.md:127` uses "certifying even 10% skips" to justify $60k-$150k/year, but a 10% skip rate is not the same as 10% trusted, buyer-accepted decision changes. Renewal depends on accepted economic receipts, not just model-labeled skips.

## FINAL JOINT NUMBERS

- **P(Amazon-class pays in 24mo): 4-6%.** Use 5% as the planning number. Residual cases are acqui-hire, research consult, or a test-starved BU, not the core motion.
- **Entry price:** $25k-$50k fixed-fee Experiment Decision Audit; quote up to $60k only with unusually strong exhaust and sponsor pull.
- **Expand ACV:** $75k-$175k/year for watchdog/certificate/post-pilot calculator after a paid audit receipt. $200k+ only after measured customer-side coverage and renewal proof.
- **First-year ICP list, ranked:**
  1. Mid-market consumer growth companies: e-commerce, subscription, gaming, marketplace; $50M-$500M revenue; 30-300+ resolved experiments; no experimentation-science bench; ideally burned by agency/vendor "wins."
  2. Traffic-scarce high-stakes verticals: consumer fintech, insurance/telco digital arms, email/CRM-heavy retail, regional streaming/OTT; tests are slow, compliance-gated, or cohort-limited.
  3. PE portfolio-operations teams: one audit relationship across multiple portfolio companies, framed as certifying operating playbooks before rollout.
- **Single most important next action:** sell 3-5 founder-led paid audits using the forward-looking dollar-denominated anti-pitch, and require a receipt: a buyer-valued reversed decision, skipped test, holdout, renewal, expansion, or referral.
- **Standing dissent retained:** I am less absolute than Fable on central experimentation-science orgs as permanent non-buyers, and I think the subscription can eventually exceed $200k in mid-market accounts. But for first-10-customer planning, Fable's lower price and demand-risk framing should govern.
