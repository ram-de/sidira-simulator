About Sidira

We read the population before you pay to find out.

Product teams aren't short on hypotheses. They're short on answers that arrive before the build commitment.

Every roadmap is a stack of bets the team can't price. A pricing change, a new onboarding, a removed feature: each one is a guess that resolves only after weeks of engineering, a launch, and a quarter of waiting on a dashboard. By the time the number comes back, the cost is sunk and the next bet is already queued.

Sidira closes that gap. We fit a customer-grounded population, synthetic agents built from real product analytics and research, then pressure-test a proposed change against it. What comes back is a directional read: how this specific population reacts, split by experiment arm, traceable down to the individual agent that adopted, hesitated, or churned.

The specimen below is one such reading. It is a record of an estimate, so it sits still, and the figures speak for the method that produced them.

What we hold to

Four laws make a reading trustworthy.

◇
The visualization is the computation.
When the product renders a population reacting, that figure is the actual run: one mark per simulated agent, not decoration added afterward. If an effect can't be read as taking a reading, it doesn't ship.
±
Inferred numbers carry their uncertainty.
A value the model estimated about people prints with its confidence interval and a calibration glyph. Recorded facts — ids, seeds, counts, dates — print plainly. The scope is mechanical, applied the same way every time.
●
Color carries meaning, never mood.
One accent marks a live reading and nothing else. Arm tints name which experiment arm: identity, not valence. A negative reaction renders in plain ink with a down-glyph rather than alarm-red, because harm is information.
◆
An ungraded reading stays hedged.
A graded reading locks and comes to rest. An ungraded one keeps its ◇ glyph and directional wording, so it never reads as ground truth. The verdict it carries is a band, stated as such.
