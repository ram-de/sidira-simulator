Reverse PRD·Continue Watching shelf·Snapshot 2026-W25·2026-06-12

A returning viewer should land on Streamly and reach the thing they were already watching in one move: no search, no scroll, no re-deciding.

Problem

Most Streamly sessions are resumptions, not discoveries. A viewer who paused episode 4 the night before does not want to be re-sold the catalog; they want to press play. When the resume path sits below promoted carousels, the cost shows up as abandoned sessions and a slow erosion of the habit that makes the service sticky.

The shelf exists to collapse that path to a single, predictable gesture. It is the first horizontally-scrolling rail on the home screen for any viewer with an in-progress title, ordered by recency of last interaction rather than editorial weight.

Who it serves

  • Returning viewers mid-series — the dominant case. Resume to the exact timecode, with a progress bar showing how far they are.
  • Multi-device viewers who start on TV and continue on mobile. Cross-device progress reconciles within about 30s of the paused session ending.
  • Kids profiles, where the shelf is filtered to age-rated in-progress titles and shows no new-and-trending bleed-through.

Behavior

Each card shows poster art, a thin progress fill at the lower edge, and a resume label (S4·E4 · 18m left). Tapping a card resumes playback at the stored timecode. The card is not a route to the title's detail page — a deliberate, debated choice, since the capability is about removing a step.

A long-press (or the overflow control on web) opens a small menu: remove from row, start over, and go to series. Removing a title is permanent for that profile and is the only way a finished title leaves the shelf early.

◇ Reading note · uncalibrated

The resume rate is a simulated estimate against the W25 persona pack, not a measured production figure. It tracks direction reliably but is not yet graded against live telemetry. Treat the ±0.05 band as the floor of what we know, not the ceiling.

Ordering and eviction

  1. Titles are sorted by last_watched_at descending; ties broken by updated_progress_at.
  2. A title is evicted when it reaches 95% complete and the next episode has been surfaced, or when manually removed.
  3. The shelf is capped at 20 titles; the tail is dropped silently rather than paginated.

Open questions

Whether a card should route to the next episode rather than resume the current one once a title is 95% complete is the live hypothesis under test. Three of the seven readings below probe exactly this seam. The population splits on it, and the split is itself the finding.
